Care Home Finance: Why Charge Counts and Financing Events Tell Different Stories

A reproducible comparison of registered charges, financing events and borrowing companies in England and Wales.

Matt Lenzie | Care Homes Finance
3 November 2026 | CHF-2026-R01 | Version 1.0

One market, three measures

In the first half of 2026, the published care-home cohort recorded 1,138 charges, 552 financing events and 802 borrowing companies. Against the same six months in 2025, charges fell by 3.3%, events rose by 0.2% and companies rose by 0.6%. A headline based only on filings would therefore describe a decline where the event measure was broadly unchanged.

This note adds a consistent comparison of all three measures and the number of charge filings per event to the Care Home Finance Report 2026 activity table. It is a secondary analysis of that report, not a separate survey or an independent verification of the underlying company filings.

Source: Care Homes Finance, seven-row lending-activity table. Changes calculated as (later / earlier - 1) x 100. Chart displays rounded values.

These are counts of recorded activity. They do not measure pounds lent, lending appetite, approval rates, pricing or the amount of capital available to an individual operator.

From 2023 to 2025, events rose 24.3%, compared with 17.8% for charges and 29.5% for borrowing companies. By January to August 2026, events were 3.8% above the matched 2025 window, while charges were 2.2% higher and companies 0.4% higher. The measures agree on the direction of that eight-month comparison but differ in scale.

The January to August and H1 windows overlap. Their counts must not be added together, and eight-month totals must not be compared with a full calendar year. September 2026 is excluded because the source treats charges as complete only to 31 August.

Fewer filings for each recorded event

Dividing charges by financing events provides a check on filing intensity. In H1 2025 there were 2.136 charges for every recorded event; in H1 2026 there were 2.062. This ratio is calculated from aggregate totals. It does not mean that each facility had that many charges, or establish the distribution across facilities.

The identity charges = events x charges per event explains how an almost unchanged event count can coexist with a lower filing count. It does not establish why filing intensity changed. The aggregate extract cannot distinguish changes in transaction structure, group organisation, security documentation or the composition of borrowers.

What an event represents

The source groups distinct combinations of lender, charge creation date and the borrower's registered postcode. That collapses multiple filings which may relate to one facility. It can also split a single facility across group addresses or combine separate deals sharing the grouping keys. The resulting count is an approximation of facilities, not a count of confirmed loan agreements.

Borrowing companies are a third unit: several companies can participate in one financing arrangement, and a company can appear in more than one event. Neither the company count nor the event count is a count of individual care homes. The parent report methodology defines the cohort and grouping rules.

How to use the findings

For an activity summary, report the event count alongside filings and borrowing companies, name the period, and retain the event definition. The H1 comparison supports a narrow statement: event activity in this observed cohort was broadly level. It cannot establish whether the same operators borrowed, whether new entrants obtained credit, or whether rates and credit terms improved.

The parent report refinancing section considers another question: the relationship between new charges and releases. Those charge-level findings should not be treated as an event-level explanation for the changes in this note. All analyses share the same Care Homes Finance source programme.

Evidence and reproduction

The accompanying package contains the unaltered lending-activity.csv extract, comparisons.csv, filing-intensity.csv, findings.json, a data dictionary, source manifest and reproduce.py. Running python3 reproduce.py regenerates the nine comparisons and seven ratios with no third-party Python dependency. Input and output files are recorded in SHA256SUMS.txt.

Download the files from the Care Homes Finance research repository, in the CHF-2026-R01 folder. The repository is maintained by the same publisher as this note.

The extract contains seven published aggregate rows, not the underlying Companies House microdata. Calculations from those totals are reproducible; the original company matching, charge selection and event deduplication are not independently reproducible from this package. No additional microdata or transaction validation is claimed.
